# Deviations from the pre-analysis plan

The plan is [`PREREGISTRATION.md`](../PREREGISTRATION.md), tagged `v0.1-prereg` (commit `0132099`).

Every change to the plan after that commit is recorded here, newest last. Each entry gives the date, what changed, why, and whether any outcome data had been seen at that point.

| 1 | 2026-11-09 | 6.1 (Repeated pulls) | Google Trends pulls 2 and 3 were run on 9 and 10 November 2026 instead of the planned 7 and 8 November. Pull 1 ran on 6 November as planned. The plan's rule of three complete pulls on three different days, at least 20 hours apart, is still met. | Scheduling: the pulls could not be run over the weekend. | Pull 1 raw files had been checked for completeness only (number of rows, partial-week flags, count of "<1" values). | 2 | 2026-11-09 | 6.1 (Sparsity rule), 7.3 | **Added analysis; the plan-as-written analysis is kept and reported as primary.** Applied as written, the sparsity rule drops "heatwave" and all three coping terms in the UK and in every nation, because these terms are near zero for most of the year and peak in summer (share of weeks with a pull-averaged value of 1 or less: 0.27–0.94). Only H3 and H5b remain testable. A second, clearly labelled analysis is added: all pre-registered Trends terms are kept (no sparsity rule), with "<1" and zero still coded 0.5; family indices then use all member terms; every other specification is unchanged, and H1–H6 are tested and Holm-adjusted as one family, as in the plan. **Floor safeguard, fixed now:** for each outcome, the share of floor-coded weeks (raw value 0.5 in the averaged series) in event weeks k = 4–8 (decay) and k = 9–12 (residue) is reported; when this share exceeds 0.5, the decay or residue estimate is labelled "floor-limited" and is not interpreted as evidence of reset. | The sparsity rule was meant to remove series too sparse to measure; for seasonal heat terms it instead removes the series the main hypotheses are about. The floor safeguard addresses the main risk of keeping them: a floor can make attention look as if it has returned to baseline. | Sparsity shares (Section 6.1 rule output) and UK Wikipedia coverage only. **No effect, coefficient or outcome plot had been produced.** This entry was committed and pushed before any analysis was run. |
| 3 | 2026-11-09 | 7.1, 7.2, 7.3 (inference) | **Added calibrated tests; the pre-registered tests are kept and reported unchanged.** A size check on simulated white noise (same weeks, fixed effects and heatwave timing; `src/08_size_check.py`) shows the pre-registered model-based tests reject far too often at the 5% level: about 15–20% for the Analysis 1 estimands (Newey–West, 8 lags) and about 21% for the Analysis 2 short-run effect (Driscoll–Kraay, 8 lags). With only a few heatwave events, these standard errors are too small. The plan's "effect present" rule already also requires the randomisation-inference p-value, which is valid by construction, but the equivalence tests (H2, H3) and the Holm step use the model-based p-values alone. **Calibrated version, fixed now:** (i) for directional and two-sided hypotheses, the randomisation-inference p-value replaces the model-based p-value; (ii) for equivalence tests, the TOST uses a standard error equal to the larger of the model-based standard error and the standard deviation of the placebo (Analysis 1) or permutation (Analysis 2) distribution; (iii) these p-values are Holm-adjusted as one family; labels follow the plan's rules. Both versions are reported, for both the plan-as-written and the deviation-2 outcomes; the paper interprets the calibrated version. | The pre-registered standard errors are not valid with so few events, which would make "reset" (equivalence) conclusions too easy to reach. | None. The size check uses simulated noise and scrambled outcomes only. **No real effect estimate had been produced.** This entry was committed and pushed before the analysis was run on real data. |
| 4 | 2026-11-09 | 7.1 (decay, residue, reference week) | **Post hoc. Added exploratory estimands; the pre-registered estimands are kept and reported.** In the end-aligned model (1b) the reference week k = −1 is the week before the episode's end week. For about half the episodes this week is still inside the heatwave (often the onset week, when attention peaks), so the pre-registered decay (mean of β4–β8) and residue (mean of β9–β12) measure attention relative to the heat itself, not relative to normal levels. The plan's "equivalent to zero" predictions (H2, H3) assumed a pre-heat reference. **Exploratory estimands:** decay and residue minus the binned pre-period coefficient β−4 of model 1b (weeks more than 3 weeks before the end week), i.e. relative to normal levels; standard errors and randomisation inference as for the other estimands. These are not confirmatory, are not Holm-adjusted with H1–H6, and are labelled as post hoc wherever reported. The pre-trend (leads) test also indicates elevated attention in the week before onset for coping terms (anticipation); this is reported, not corrected. | Specification error in the plan: the chosen reference week does not represent normal attention for the end-aligned model. | **Yes.** Provisional results on pulls 1 and 2 (all estimands, H1–H6) had been seen. Pull 3 had not been downloaded. This entry is post hoc and is reported as such. |
